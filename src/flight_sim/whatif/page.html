Geometry: the .ork now against the rocket RASAero was run for

How the numbers are made. Size, mass and launch conditions come from the .ork. Lift and drag come from the team's RASAero table (the CSV). If the .ork no longer matches the rocket RASAero was run for, the model corrects the table (see the geometry table above). The 6-DOF column is the full sim flown on the RASAero table. The OpenRocket column is what is saved in the .ork. The gaps between the columns show how far to trust the model. After apogee the rocket falls as a point mass under the descent named in the chips above, in the same wind.

What it does not do

The motor is the newest motor file in the thrust-curve folder. A longer or heavier rocket does not get more propellant. The descent is a stand-in: the .ork has no parachutes, so it uses the Sol Invictus recovery until SRT14 has its own, and OpenRocket's landing figures are left out for the same reason. Not checked: fin flutter, fin loads, rail-button loads and roll. The model is flat (up, downrange, pitch), so crosswind, coning and roll coupling are only in the 3D flight. If the geometry table shows a big difference (over about 25% in a fin size, a different fin count, nose shape or body diameter, or a new boat-tail), run Update CSV.

Update CSV
Before you press Step 1:
  • Open RASAero II with the current rocket.
  • Save it over rasaero.CDX1.
  • Click inside RASAero, so it is the last window you used.
  • Come back here and press Step 1. Then do not touch the keyboard or mouse. To stop it, move the mouse into a screen corner.

  2. Turns the files into the CSV, rebuilds the model, reruns the 6-DOF sim and shows the new page.
